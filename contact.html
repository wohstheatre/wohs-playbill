Friendly, professional outreach

Contact a business with confidence.

Use these examples as a guide. You do not need to memorize them word for word. Make them sound natural while staying polite and professional.

Quick tip: Take a breath and be yourself. Business owners know you are a student. Clear, friendly, and respectful matters more than saying every sentence perfectly.

💡 A quick reminder

You don't have to memorize these examples word for word. Think of them as conversation starters...not a script.

Business owners know you're a student. What they'll remember most is that you were friendly, respectful, and genuine.

Email

Subject: Supporting West Orange High School Theatre

Hello [Name],

My name is [Student Name], and I am a student with West Orange High School Theatre.

We are inviting local businesses to be part of our upcoming theatre season by sponsoring our annual playbill. Sponsorships help provide costumes, sets, props, and other opportunities for our student performers.

Sponsorships start at $75, and we would love to feature your business this season. I would be happy to send you our sponsorship packet or answer any questions.

Thank you for your time and for considering supporting local students and the arts!

Sincerely,
[Student Name]

Phone conversation

“Hi! My name is [Name], and I am a student with West Orange High School Theatre. May I speak with the person who handles advertising, marketing, or community sponsorships?”

“We are inviting local businesses to support our theatre season through an advertisement in our annual playbill. Sponsorships start at $75. May I email you the information?”

If they say yes:
“Wonderful! What is the best email address to use?”

If they say no:
“I understand. Thank you so much for your time. Have a great day!”

Before hanging up: Write down the person’s name, email address, and any date they ask you to follow up.

In-person conversation

“Hi! My name is [Name], and I am a student with West Orange High School Theatre.”

“We are inviting local businesses to support our theatre program through an advertisement in our annual playbill. Is the owner or manager available, or is there someone who handles sponsorships or advertising?”

If they are unavailable:
“No problem! May I leave some information for them? When would be a good time for me to follow up?”

Be prepared to leave the sponsorship packet. Stay friendly and thank the person helping you.

Good follow-up habits

  • Be friendly, confident, and respectful.
  • Write down the contact person’s name and information.
  • If they ask you to follow up, record the date.
  • Do not promise ad space or placement unless you know it is available.
  • If you do not know an answer, say that you will check and follow up.
  • Report the result after every conversation, even if the business declines.
  • Always thank the business for its time.
